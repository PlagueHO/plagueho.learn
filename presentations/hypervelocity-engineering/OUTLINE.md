## Audience outcome

Attendees should leave able to explain HVE as an integrated operating model, identify where their organization is missing one of its four ingredients, and select a valuable workflow in which to trial the model with a small multidisciplinary team.

## Narrative thesis

Hypervelocity Engineering combines small expert teams, design thinking, proven production starting points, and AI across the lifecycle to deliver valuable, trustworthy software in days or weeks—not by relaxing engineering rigor, but by making rigor faster and continuous.

## Learning objectives

1. Explain why AI changes the engineering operating model rather than merely accelerating coding.
1. Describe the four ingredients of HVE and how each constrains the others.
1. Use design thinking and continuous user evidence to keep velocity aligned with business value.
1. Recognize production starting points, Responsible AI, security, testing, and observability as accelerators rather than late-stage gates.
1. Understand HVE-Core and RPI as examples of how AI agents and tools can operationalize HVE.
1. Lead a measured HVE adoption experiment based on value, speed, quality, trust, and learning.

## Slide outline

### 1. Hypervelocity Engineering

- **Takeaway**: HVE makes production rigor faster and continuous.
- **Visual**: Dark hero with four connected ingredients and the session route.
- **Content**: Title, thesis, presenter, audience, and 75-minute duration.
- **Speaker intent**: Establish that the session is about an engineering operating model, not a tool or an FDE role.
- **Duration**: 1 min.

### 2. AI collapsed the distance from intent to implementation

- **Takeaway**: Generating software is no longer the slowest part of delivery.
- **Visual**: Two-stage compression diagram: idea → code versus intent → trusted outcome.
- **Evidence**: AI accelerates implementation, but context, judgment, validation, and adoption still bound outcomes.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 3. Speed without purpose accelerates the wrong outcome

- **Takeaway**: The opportunity is rapid learning; the trap is rapidly industrializing the wrong answer.
- **Visual**: Purpose-and-rigor matrix contrasting prototypes, uncontrolled generation, traditional delivery, and HVE.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 4. HVE changes the operating model

- **Takeaway**: HVE replaces large linear handoffs with a small expert team continuously learning and delivering.
- **Visual**: Traditional delivery rail compared with a continuous HVE loop.
- **Duration**: 4 min.

### 5. Four ingredients create one system

- **Takeaway**: HVE fails when any ingredient operates alone.
- **Visual**: Four linked nodes around “valuable, trustworthy outcomes.”
- **Ingredients**:
  - Tight multidisciplinary teams with deep domain expertise.
  - Design thinking methods that create business value.
  - Proven, mission-critical, production starting points.
  - AI agents and tools across the full lifecycle.
- **Duration**: 4 min.

### 6. Small expert teams reduce coordination latency

- **Takeaway**: A compact team with decision-making expertise can turn feedback into action without handoff queues.
- **Visual**: Three-to-four-person crew surrounding the user and outcome.
- **Caveat**: The 3–4 versus 10+ comparison is indicative Microsoft internal engineering experience, not a staffing formula.
- **Duration**: 4 min.

### 7. Design thinking keeps velocity pointed at value

- **Takeaway**: HVE learns what is valuable before scaling what is buildable.
- **Visual**: Problem → Solution → Implementation spaces with evidence and decision gates.
- **Methods**: Scope and research; synthesis and concepts; prototypes, user testing, and iteration.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 8. The factory floor changed the backlog

- **Takeaway**: Direct evidence changes both the problem and the economics of the solution.
- **Visual**: Four observations transforming into four backlog decisions.
- **Scenario**:
  - Gloves and greasy fingers invalidate keyboard-first interaction.
  - Real worker questions expose reliability gaps.
  - Undisclosed repair manuals reveal missing knowledge.
  - Timed prototypes test whether the new path is genuinely better.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 9. Start from production, not from zero

- **Takeaway**: Proven starting points move effort from scaffolding to differentiated value.
- **Visual**: Production foundation supporting business-specific layers.
- **Evidence**: Microsoft HVE Accelerators Hub describes proven starting points for AI-driven development.
- **Duration**: 4 min.

### 10. AI belongs across the lifecycle

- **Takeaway**: Code generation is one activity in a much larger engineering system.
- **Visual**: Lifecycle from discovery through operations with AI roles and human decisions.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 11. Continuous loops replace staged handoffs

- **Takeaway**: Envisioning, exploration, design, development, and validation happen as one learning loop.
- **Visual**: Six-stage loop with user evidence returning to intent.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 12. Hypervelocity does not trade away rigor

- **Takeaway**: Security, testing, observability, maintainability, and governance are built into every increment.
- **Visual**: Quality rails running beneath the lifecycle.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 13. Responsible AI is designed in

- **Takeaway**: Trust is an engineering property, not a launch checklist.
- **Visual**: Five decisions: intentional use, transparency, risk controls, outcome quality, and production readiness.
- **Evidence**: Microsoft Responsible AI principles and HVE-Core Transparency Note.
- **Duration**: 3 min.

### 14. Internal experience shows the shape—not a guarantee

- **Takeaway**: HVE changes delivery economics, but outcomes depend on context.
- **Visual**: Three direct-labeled figures with a persistent qualification.
- **Figures**:
  - 2–3× velocity increase.
  - 3–4 experts compared with 10+ people.
  - 30%+ code written by AI.
- **Qualification**: Indicative Microsoft internal engineering experience; not a universal benchmark or target.
- **Duration**: 2 min.

### 15. HVE-Core is one production starting point

- **Takeaway**: HVE-Core helps teams adopt repeatable agentic engineering patterns without starting from zero.
- **Visual**: GitHub Copilot as the execution surface; HVE-Core provides reusable agents, skills, instructions, and workflows.
- **Caveat**: HVE-Core is opinionated and rapidly evolving; treat it as patterns and learning, not a mandatory platform.
- **Duration**: 2 min.

### 16. Context quality bounds output quality

- **Takeaway**: Better models cannot compensate for missing domain evidence, conventions, or acceptance criteria.
- **Visual**: Context budget showing relevant evidence, instructions, history, and noise.
- **Practice**: Keep context intentional; persist findings in artifacts; reset between distinct phases when appropriate.
- **Duration**: 2 min.

### 17. RPI creates an evidence chain

- **Takeaway**: Research → Plan → Implement → Review turns uncertainty into a traceable engineering decision.
- **Visual**: Four-stage rail with outputs and human checkpoints.
- **Duration**: 2 min.

### 18. Demo: secure Marginalia’s analysis pipeline

- **Takeaway**: The demo starts from user and business risk, not from a coding task.
- **Visual**: Untrusted guidance and document text entering a Foundry-backed analysis pipeline.
- **Outcome**: Reduce prompt-injection exposure while preserving editorial value, latency, maintainability, and testability.
- **Duration**: 1 min.

### 19. Demo: Research and Plan

- **Takeaway**: Evidence becomes an implementation contract before code changes.
- **Visual**: Live VS Code demonstration with fallback artifact excerpts.
- **Actions**:
  - Research internal flow and authoritative external mitigations.
  - Select one layered approach.
  - Create a phased, testable plan linked to evidence.
- **Duration**: 7 min.

### 20. Demo: Implement and Review

- **Takeaway**: The implementation follows the contract and the review tests both behavior and intent.
- **Visual**: Live VS Code demonstration with fallback changes and review excerpts.
- **Actions**:
  - Apply prompt isolation, guidance relocation, boundary constraints, and tests.
  - Review against research, plan, codebase conventions, and validation results.
- **Duration**: 7 min.

### 21. The demo used all four HVE ingredients

- **Takeaway**: RPI alone did not produce the outcome; the operating model did.
- **Visual**: Four ingredients mapped to concrete demo evidence.
- **Duration**: 1 min.

### 22. Lead adoption as an operating-model change

- **Takeaway**: Begin with one valuable workflow and one empowered team, then standardize what works.
- **Visual**: Three horizons: prove, operationalize, scale.
- **Actions**:
  - Choose a measurable value stream.
  - Form a small multidisciplinary crew.
  - Select a proven production starting point.
  - Apply AI across the lifecycle.
  - Capture evidence and improve the system.
- **Duration**: 5 min.

### 23. Measure outcomes, not AI activity

- **Takeaway**: AI usage is an input; business value and trustworthy delivery are outcomes.
- **Visual**: Balanced scorecard.
- **Measures**:
  - Value: user and business outcome.
  - Speed: idea-to-evidence and evidence-to-production.
  - Quality: escaped defects, rework, and reliability.
  - Trust: security, explainability, and control effectiveness.
  - Learning: validated assumptions and reusable knowledge.
- **Duration**: 5 min.

### 24. The HVE leadership challenge

- **Takeaway**: Pick one important workflow and redesign the system around all four ingredients.
- **Visual**: Dark close with one action, resources, presenter links, and Q&A.
- **Call to action**: “Do not add AI to the old process. Build a better engineering system.”
- **Duration**: 5 min, including questions.

## Demonstration contract

The only main-path demonstration uses [PlagueHO/marginalia](https://github.com/PlagueHO/marginalia) and follows one RPI sequence:

1. **Research**: Trace untrusted inputs through the analysis pipeline and verify mitigation guidance.
1. **Plan**: Define a layered, testable increment with explicit acceptance criteria.
1. **Implement**: Follow the plan and track changes.
1. **Review**: Validate behavior, security intent, tests, and adherence to the evidence.

The live path is 14–16 minutes. Pre-generated artifacts provide a deterministic fallback. The detailed script remains in `DEMO.md`.

## Evidence policy

- Attribute “proven starting points” to the [Microsoft HVE Accelerators Hub](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/industry/playbook/).
- Use the current [HVE-Core documentation](https://microsoft.github.io/hve-core/docs/) for Design Thinking, lifecycle, and RPI descriptions.
- Use [Microsoft Responsible AI](https://www.microsoft.com/ai/responsible-ai) and the [HVE-Core Transparency Note](https://github.com/microsoft/hve-core/blob/main/TRANSPARENCY-NOTE.md) for trust and responsibility claims.
- Label all internal-experience figures on-slide and in notes. Do not present them as externally validated benchmarks.
- Avoid version-sensitive inventory counts unless the count itself is necessary to the message.
- Keep FDE-specific practices outside this presentation.

## Presenter guardrails

- Say “HVE operating model,” not “HVE toolchain.”
- Say “one proven starting point,” not “the HVE platform.”
- Use internal figures to illustrate the shape of change, never to promise an outcome.
- Connect every tool example back to teams, user evidence, production foundations, and engineering rigor.
- If time is lost, shorten the live demo; do not cut the operating-model or measurement sections.
